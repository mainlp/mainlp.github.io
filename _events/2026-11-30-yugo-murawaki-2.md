title: "Reconciling Vertical Inheritance and Horizontal Diffusion in Dialect Evolution"
abstract: "<p>How can we reconstruct the evolutionary history of dialects from lexical data? Traditional dialectology has emphasized patterns of horizontal diffusion, while some computational phylogenetic studies have applied tree models to dialect data without adequately considering whether their assumptions of vertical inheritance hold.</p>

<p>In this talk, I will discuss my attempts to reconcile these two perspectives. First, I use simulation and visualization to demonstrate the problems that can arise from naively applying Bayesian phylogenetic methods to dialect data. I then turn to the challenge of jointly modeling vertical inheritance and horizontal diffusion. Inspired by historical linguists who seek to identify shared innovations as evidence of genealogical relationships amid overlapping geographical patterns, I take a geography-based approach. I first introduce a Bayesian model that infers potentially overlapping geographical distributions from observed lexical data. I then discuss ongoing work to constrain some of these latent distributions to form a genealogical tree.</p>"
speaker: Yugo Murawaki<br/>(Associate Professor, Graduate School of Informatics, Kyoto University, Japan)
bio: "<p>Yugo Murawaki is an Associate Professor at the Graduate School of Informatics, Kyoto University, Japan. He is currently visiting the University of Passau, Germany. His research interests include natural language processing, computational historical linguistics, and the human-likeness of large language models.</p>"

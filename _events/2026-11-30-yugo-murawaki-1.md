title: "Can LLMs Serve as Human Proxies?"
abstract: "<p>Experiments involving human participants are often slow, costly, and difficult to repeat or scale up. If LLMs could serve as reliable proxies for humans, they could greatly accelerate research in fields such as education. But how can we evaluate whether LLMs are sufficiently human-like for this purpose?</p>

<p>In this talk, I will discuss two lines of research exploring this question. First, we investigate whether prompting-based role playing can reliably simulate people with different personality traits. Using established psychometric instruments based on the Big Five personality traits, we identify limitations of this approach, including its vulnerability to manipulation through conversational context. Second, we explore the challenge of plausibly simulating people who lack certain knowledge or abilities, focusing on second-language acquisition as a case study. I will conclude by discussing the broader challenge of simulating human diversity, particularly when established classifications and evaluation methods are unavailable.</p>"
speaker: Yugo Murawaki<br/>(Associate Professor, Graduate School of Informatics, Kyoto University, Japan)
bio: "<p>Yugo Murawaki is an Associate Professor at the Graduate School of Informatics, Kyoto University, Japan. He is currently visiting the University of Passau, Germany. His research interests include natural language processing, computational historical linguistics, and the human-likeness of large language models.</p>"
